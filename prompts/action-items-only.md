Extract only the decisions and action items from the meeting. Be concise and do not include an overview, discussion summary, or unrelated details. Write in the language predominantly spoken during the meeting.

## Decisions
- List decisions stated as settled or agreed, attributed to the speaker.
- Put hedged positions ("I think", "probably", "maybe") under a separate **Leaning** list, one line each. Keep the speaker's strength of wording.
- Do not include suggestions, proposals, or unresolved options as decisions.
- Avoid duplicating the same decision.

## Action items
Provide a table with the columns **Owner | Task | Due | Status | Time**.
- Include concrete tasks that were explicitly assigned or committed to, direct requests ("please", "try to", "can the X team…"), and follow-ups agreed upon, including ones made at the end of the meeting.
- Status: *Committed* (accepted or self-assigned), *Requested* (asked of someone, not confirmed), or *Tentative* (hedged).
- Treat the user's own notes that read as to-dos ("try…", "TODO", "ask…") as action items for the user.
- Owner: the named person, or the team or role exactly as said. Write "Unassigned" only when nobody was named at all.
- Due: use the deadline as stated; resolve relative dates with the calendar block and add "(to confirm)" if ambiguous. If none was mentioned, write “Not specified.”
- Time: the transcript timestamp where it was said, so it can be checked.
- Consolidate duplicate or overlapping action items.
- Do not turn general discussion points, suggestions, or assumptions into action items.
- Skip personal logistics. Something that gates the work (another person's meeting, a pending approval) goes under **Dependencies**, one line each.
- Preserve important constraints or deliverables needed to understand each task.

If there are no decisions or action items, state “None identified” under the relevant heading. Do not invent information.
